    Legal & election framework

    Summaries only, not legal advice. Check the text of the law and current COMELEC resolutions.

    How the schedule changed

    1. August 2025 — RA 12232: Postponed the December 2025 BSKE to the first Monday of November 2026 and set a four-year term.
    2. September 24, 2026 — RA 12326: Cancelled the November 2, 2026 elections, fixed the term at five years and set the next elections for the second Monday of November 2028, and every five years after.
    3. November 6, 2026: The Supreme Court upheld RA 12326. A motion for partial reconsideration was filed on November 10, 2026.
    RA 12326 key provisions
    • Sec. 1 — Term and limits: Five-year term. Barangay officials may serve no more than two (2) consecutive terms in the same position (SK officials: one term). Voluntary renunciation does not interrupt a full term.
    • Election date: Second Monday of November 2028 (November 13, 2028), and every five years after.
    • Sec. 4-B — Training: DILG, with TESDA, COMELEC and state universities and colleges (SUCs), to institutionalize continuing training and capacity-building for barangay officials and SK members.
    • Sec. 5 — Transitory rule: Because terms were extended by two years, incumbents are treated as having completed one term. Those serving their last permitted term in the same position cannot run for that position in November 2028.
    • Sec. 6: Incumbents are not exempt from pending or future administrative, civil or criminal cases.
    RA 7160, Sec. 39 — Qualifications
    • Citizen of the Philippines.
    • Registered voter in the barangay where you will run.
    • Resident of that barangay for at least one (1) year immediately before election day (cutoff: November 13, 2027).
    • Able to read and write Filipino or any other local language or dialect.
    • At least 18 years old on election day.
    RA 7160, Sec. 40 — Disqualifications
    • Final sentence for a moral-turpitude offense, or one punishable by 1+ year imprisonment, within two years after serving.
    • Removed from office through an administrative case.
    • Final conviction for violating the oath of allegiance.
    • Dual citizenship.
    • Fugitive from justice.
    • Permanent resident or immigrant abroad, without the required waiver.
    • Insane or feeble-minded.

    The Omnibus Election Code adds other grounds. Barangay elections are non-partisan.

      First 100 days & Katarungang Pambarangay playbook

      Based on RA 7160 Sec. 389 and related provisions. Verify the current details with DILG.

      1. Days 1–15: Set up your team. Appoint the Barangay Secretary and Treasurer (with the concurrence required by law), and organize your barangay tanods. Constitute the Lupong Tagapamayapa within 15 days from the start of your term.
      2. Days 1–30: Take stock. Review the barangay's budget, cash position, pending contracts, programs and records. Meet the Sangguniang Barangay and the SK chairperson.
      3. Days 30–60: Convene and plan. Organize the Barangay Development Council, which you chair, and begin the Barangay Development Plan. Activate the peace and order, anti-drug (BADAC) and disaster (BDRRMC) committees.
      4. Days 60–100: Show results. Hold a Barangay Assembly, publish your plan and budget priorities, and attend the training that DILG, TESDA, COMELEC and SUCs run under RA 12326 Sec. 4-B.
      Executive powers (RA 7160 Sec. 389)
      • Enforce laws and ordinances within the barangay.
      • Sign contracts for the barangay, with the authorization of the Sangguniang Barangay.
      • Maintain public order and call on the police when needed.
      • Preside over Sangguniang Barangay sessions and barangay assemblies.
      • Prepare the annual executive budget proposal.
      • Organize an emergency response group for calamities and disorder.
      Katarungang Pambarangay timeline
      1. 15 days: mediation by the Punong Barangay.
      2. 15 days: conciliation by the Pangkat ng Tagapagkasundo if mediation fails (extendable by up to 15 more days).
      3. 10 days: a settlement becomes final and executory unless repudiated.

      Some disputes are exempt, for example those between residents of different cities, or offenses punishable by more than one year of imprisonment or a fine above ₱5,000.

      Barangay Assembly

      The Barangay Assembly, made up of residents who are registered voters, meets at least twice a year. You call it to order and report on the barangay's finances and programs.

      Honoraria & benefits

      Barangay officials receive honoraria fixed by the Sangguniang Panlungsod within the law, plus statutory benefits. Ask the City Treasurer or DILG for the current rates, since they change by ordinance and circular.
