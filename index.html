Legal & election framework

Summaries only, not legal advice. Always check the text of the law and current COMELEC resolutions.

How the schedule changed

  1. Before 2025: The BSKE had been set for December 2025.
  2. August 2025 — RA 12232: Postponed the December 2025 BSKE to the first Monday of November 2026 (November 2, 2026), set a four-year term, a three-consecutive-term limit for barangay officials and a one-term limit for SK officials. Incumbents serve in a holdover capacity until successors are elected and qualified, and officials on their third consecutive term could not run for the same post in 2026.
  3. September 24, 2026 — RA 12326: Fixed the term of barangay and SK officials at five years, cancelled the November 2, 2026 elections and rescheduled them to November 2028. Incumbents continue in a holdover capacity until their successors are elected and qualified.
  4. November 6, 2026: The Supreme Court upheld the constitutionality of RA 12326. A motion for partial reconsideration was filed on November 10, 2026.
Open questions to verify with COMELEC: the exact 2028 election date, the filing period for Certificates of Candidacy, and how term limits are counted under RA 12326. This guide does not guess at them.

RA 7160, Sec. 39 — Qualifications

  • Citizen of the Philippines.
  • Registered voter in the barangay where you intend to be elected.
  • Resident of that barangay for at least one (1) year immediately preceding the day of the election.
  • Able to read and write Filipino or any other local language or dialect.
  • At least eighteen (18) years old on election day.

RA 7160, Sec. 40 — Disqualifications

  • Sentenced by final judgment for an offense involving moral turpitude, or punishable by one year or more of imprisonment, within two years after serving sentence.
  • Removed from office as a result of an administrative case.
  • Convicted by final judgment for violating the oath of allegiance to the Republic.
  • Dual citizenship.
  • Fugitive from justice in criminal or non-political cases.
  • Permanent resident of, or immigrant to, a foreign country (unless the right of abode is waived as the law requires).
  • Insane or feeble-minded.

The Omnibus Election Code (Secs. 12 and 68) adds further grounds, such as certain election offenses. Barangay elections are non-partisan.

Pasig City barangay directory

Pasig is a highly urbanized city of 30 barangays with a single (lone) congressional district, so there is no District 1 / District 2 split for filing.

COMELEC office: File and ask questions at the Office of the Election Officer, Pasig City (COMELEC). Confirm the address, office hours and filing schedule on comelec.gov.ph or with the office directly, because filing periods are set per election.

    Population figures are from the 2024 Census of Population (POPCEN) as published in public barangay directories, shown only for the five largest barangays. Voter counts change and are not included.

    Powers, duties & compensation of a Punong Barangay

    Based on RA 7160 (Local Government Code), Sec. 389 and related provisions.

    Executive powers (Sec. 389)

    • Enforce all laws and ordinances applicable within the barangay.
    • Negotiate, enter into and sign contracts for the barangay, with the authorization of the Sangguniang Barangay.
    • Maintain public order, and call on the police or other government units when needed.
    • Organize and lead an emergency group to respond to calamities, crime and disorder.
    • Preside over Sangguniang Barangay sessions (with a vote only to break a tie) and over barangay assemblies.
    • Prepare and submit the annual executive budget proposal to the Sangguniang Barangay.
    • Appoint or replace the barangay treasurer and secretary, subject to the law.
    • Ensure delivery of basic services and promote the general welfare, including cleanliness, health and anti-drug efforts.

    Katarungang Pambarangay

    The Punong Barangay chairs the Lupong Tagapamayapa and mediates disputes between residents. Most disputes within the same city must go through barangay conciliation before they can be filed in court.

    • Mediation by the Punong Barangay: 15 days.
    • Pangkat ng Tagapagkasundo, if mediation fails: a further 15 days (extendable by up to 15 more).
    • A settlement has the force of a final court judgment after 10 days, unless repudiated.
    • Certain disputes are exempt, for example those involving parties in different cities, or offenses punishable by more than one year of imprisonment or a fine above ₱5,000.

    Development & peace and order

    • Chairs the Barangay Development Council (BDC), which prepares the Barangay Development Plan.
    • Leads the Barangay Peace and Order Committee and the Barangay Anti-Drug Abuse Council (BADAC).
    • Heads the Barangay Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Committee (BDRRMC).
    • Oversees the barangay's share of internal revenue allotment (IRA) and local funds, subject to COA rules.

    Honoraria & benefits

    Barangay officials are not paid a fixed salary. They receive honoraria set by the Sangguniang Panlungsod within the limits of the law, plus statutory benefits such as allowances and insurance coverage.

    The amount varies by city and by the barangay's resources. Ask the City Treasurer or the DILG field office in Pasig for the current rates, because they are changed by ordinance and national circulars.
